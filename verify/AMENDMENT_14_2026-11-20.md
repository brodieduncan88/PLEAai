# Amendment 14, 20 November 2026

Private working material. Times are UTC.

This is internal work by the author of the code, under the owner's direction. It is not an independent validation.

## What it does

It restates two tests, and changes no code of the service. `DECLARED_AMEND14.md` states the changes, the expected outcomes,
the development work and what stays open.

- **F17**, which shows that two failures whose notices would have the same file name both reach the engineer, made its case
  by timing alone, and failed in the official run of Amendment 12 without having tested anything. It now shows the naming
  with no clock at all; makes the case through the service by two jobs sending at the same moment; and says for itself
  whether the case was made: COLLISION_HANDLED, COLLISION_MISHANDLED, or PRECONDITION_NOT_MET, which is a failure to test
  and never a pass.
- **R28**, which Amendment 13 declared, was reviewed, and could not see five kinds of fault in the reading it tests. It now
  counts connections where the driver opens them, cuts a reading at a report's own decision, and changes the store by hand
  to show what the reading must not take: another budget's rows, a result for a run that had no effect, a result that is
  no value; and that it does not fail on a run with no rows.

## A correction to the amendment of Amendment 12

`verify/AMENDMENT_12_2026-11-19.md` says of F17, "When F17 fails, each of its runs takes about a second", and "Why is still
not known". Neither is so now. No run took a second: each took about half of one, and the pair ended a few hundredths of
a second after the second did. The cause is known: F17 had about a tenth of a second to spare, the sandbox takes about
0.07 s more for each run, and each run on a baseline was slower than the one before (repaired by Amendment 13). That
document is not changed. F17's failure in the official run of Amendment 12 stands as it was reported.

## What changed on the way

F17 did not change after it was restated. Five things were planted against it: with four defects of the notices it said
the case was made and mishandled; with the two jobs made to send a second apart it said PRECONDITION_NOT_MET and failed.

R28 went through two more forms. An independent review of the form Amendment 13 declared found five things it could not
see, all of medium weight; the second form was written to see them. A pass of 22 deliberate defects on it caught 19: with
two, R28 stopped with an error where it should have failed a check, and one, the results of every budget read, did not
show, because of the order in which rows happen to be read. The third form mends both, and a pass on it caught all 22,
with the control passing.

So two reviews and three passes of deliberate defects have each found something R28 could not see. That it now sees
everything is not claimed. The restated F17 and the restated R28 were read only by their author.

## What stays open

The declaration's "What stays open" section is the full list. The main items are:

- F17's case through the service is still made by timing, with more to spare: the pair ends 0.6 to 0.95 s into the second.
  On a slower machine it could not be made; F17 would then fail and say so.
- R28 changes the store by hand, to put there what the service would not write. It shows what the reading does with such
  rows, not that they can arise.
- What Amendment 13 left open is still open.
