# Amendment 8, 17 November 2026

Private working material. Times are UTC.

## What it repairs

What Amendment 7 left silent in two defects of the engineer's defect register, both pilot blockers in the review pack,
section 3. D3: nothing checked, when the gate started or when a job was enrolled, that the gate could write the engineer's
inbox, so the fault was found by the first failure. D1: the journal's size limit was shared, so one job could fill it and every
job was then refused; and the refusal went only to the caller. `DECLARED_AMEND8.md` states the changes, the expected outcomes,
the development work and what stays open.

In short:

- **The gate proves that it can deliver.** The gate's tool writes a small file into each baseline's inbox and removes it: as it
  starts, on every run, and on a look of its own. It keeps a dated proof of what it found. A proof is good for 120 seconds.
- **A job is opened, and a new run admitted, only on a good proof.** Enrolment and a move to a revision wait for the proof and
  refuse without it, changing nothing. The front door refuses a new run with 503 `DELIVERY_UNPROVEN`, before anything is
  journaled, run or counted. A design whose run is finished is still answered. A run that an earlier request began is not
  denied by the refusal: it is said to be on the record with no verdict yet. The engineer's console shows the state.
- **Each baseline has its own share of the journal**, 64 MiB unless set. A baseline at its share is refused with 507
  `STORAGE_FULL`; the others run.
- **The engineer is told of a refusal.** A refusal for want of room, or for an unproven inbox, writes one notice for each reason
  on the day to the engineer of the refused job's baseline, and the caller is told whether the engineer could be sent it. A
  notice the inbox could not take is kept in the service's own store, and the inbox gets its copy once it can.

## What changed on the way

The first run of the nearest tests found F19 failing, as expected: it sends a new design at once after the inbox is put back,
and this amendment refuses that design until the gate has proved the inbox again. F19 is restated, with its old expectations
kept in the Amendment 7 results.

The code went through three forms before the declaration. The author's own read found two faults in the first. An independent
review of the first form found nine things, and a second independent review, of the fixes for those, found eight more. The
most serious were a design let through on a bad proof because a run of it had been begun; a baseline whose folder was gone
refused with the engineer told nothing; and, in the fixes, a refusal that said nothing had been run of a design whose run was
already on the record. Those three are fixed. Several findings are left open, in whole or in part, and each is stated in the
declaration. The third form, which carries the fixes for the second review, was not itself read by a reviewer.

The planted-defect pass on the third form caught 56 of 56 deliberate defects, with the control passing. Twice on the way that
pass showed a test that stopped with an error where a check should have failed, and the tests were changed so that a check
fails: R22 and F29 after the second form, and F29 again, by one line, after the third. The declaration records what was run
again after each change.

## What stays open

The declaration's "What stays open" section is the full list. The main items are:

- The engineer is still told by notice or by asking. A notice of a refusal is written by the service that refuses. The
  statement signed by the witness, which is the repair for D2 and D11 and for telling a current record from an old one (D7), is
  not in this amendment.
- For up to 30 seconds after an inbox goes bad, and for up to 120 seconds after the gate stops, the proof still reads as good.
  A run admitted in that time is answered by Amendment 7's fallback, or fails because the gate cannot be reached.
- A run cut off while the proof is not good is not reported as interrupted until the proof is good and its design is asked for
  again. Until then the refusal says that the run is on the record with no verdict yet, and the console shows it as PENDING.
- A second episode of refusals on the same day, for the same reason, writes no new notice.
- The proof is an unsigned file. It shows that the service is set up and working, and is no defence against someone who can
  write the service's home.
- Real accounts and a real full disk were not used. Each is simulated.
- The share is a default, and no archive or rotation of the journal exists.
- D2, D5 to D8 and D11 to D13 are not repaired; the gap-table rows on the keep list (G4, G7, G8, G10, G11) are not in this
  amendment.
