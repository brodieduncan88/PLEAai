# Amendment 7, 16 November 2026

Private working material. Times are UTC.

## What it repairs

Five defects in the engineer's defect register, all pilot blockers in the review pack, section 3: D1 (the front door's
journal has no size limit, and a full disk stops notices for every job), D2 (a notice that cannot be written leaves a run
counted with no verdict and no notice, and can leave a part-written notice), D3 (with the console and the gate under two
accounts, a failing run stops with no verdict and no notice when the gate cannot write the console's inbox), D4 (a card
whose stated limits are not text can be signed, and then a failing run gets no verdict and no notice), and D11 (a run cut
off when the gate host dies is counted INTERRUPTED and gets no notice). `DECLARED_AMEND7.md` states the changes, the
expected outcomes, the development work and what stays open.

In short:

- **A verdict is returned, and its notice has a state.** A notice goes to the engineer's inbox; if the inbox cannot take it,
  to the service's own `undelivered/` store, which the engineer's console lists; if neither can, the answer says so. A notice
  is written whole or not at all, and never twice under one name.
- **A card with non-text stated limits is refused**, at baselining and at every run.
- **The journal is bounded, and the disk keeps room for notices.** The front door refuses a new run, before any attempt, with
  507, when the journal would pass its size limit or when the disk would drop below the space kept for notices. The room check
  applies to every submission, a re-ask included.
- **A run cut off is noticed once**, with the design, the statement that no verdict was recorded, and that it will not be run
  again. The gate signs such a run's UNKNOWN at the next ask for its design; until then the run is PENDING and not noticed.

## What changed on the way

The first full run of the front suite found F17 failing on a ledger field this amendment had changed, and F19 asserting the
known limit that this amendment repairs. F17 was fixed in the code. F19 is restated to the repaired behaviour, with the old
expectations kept in the Amendment 6 results. Two new checks found faults in the tests themselves, and both were corrected;
the declaration records that. An independent review, before the declaration, found seven things. One was a real high-severity
fault, a verdict lost when the ledger's error output also failed; it was fixed. Five more were fixed, and one partly: a
cut-off run is noticed when the gate signs it, at the next ask for the design, and that is the gate's protocol. Its details are
in the declaration.

## What stays open

The declaration's "What stays open" section is the full list. The main items are:

- D5 to D8 are not repaired; the gap-table rows on the keep list (G4, G7, G8, G10, G11) are not in this amendment.
- A run nobody asks about again stays PENDING and is not noticed, because the gate signs its UNKNOWN only at the next ask.
- The journal's limits are defaults the operator sets; at the limit the front door refuses runs, and no archive procedure
  exists. The engineer is not notified of a storage refusal.
- A notice is written before the gate host records the verdict; a host that dies between the two leaves a notice of a verdict
  the record does not hold. This order was as before and is not changed.
- Who can read the `undelivered/` store depends on the deployment's permissions, and was not tested across accounts.
