# Amendment 12, 19 November 2026

Private working material. Times are UTC.

## What it repairs

A declared check is code the customer supplies, and the service ran it as a separate process in an empty folder, under its
own account, with its network, its files and its processes in reach. This is D19 in the defect register and release gate
G2, "strong disposable sandbox for customer-supplied checks". `DECLARED_AMEND12.md` states the changes, the expected
outcomes, the development work and what stays open.

In short, each run of a check now gets a sandbox of its own (`verify/sandbox.py`), made for it and thrown away after it:

- **Identities of its own.** Two host ids chosen for that run alone. The first of them, not root, makes new user, mount,
  process, network, IPC and host-name namespaces, so what the kernel counts for each user is the run's.
- **Nothing of the machine but /usr.** A root in memory, holding /usr read-only (less /usr/local, /usr/src and the site and
  dist packages), a /work and a /tmp of 64 MiB each, and three devices. No network, no /etc, no /proc, none of the service's
  files.
- **Limits for the run as a whole.** Memory, process and freezer control groups: 1 GiB and 64 processes. Per-process limits
  on CPU time, memory, file size, open files and pending signals; 1 MiB of each output; the run's time.
- **The check is never the first process.** That process is the sandbox's own: it starts the check, reaps what it leaves,
  and ends when it ends, and its end, or its launcher's, ends every process of the run. The launcher dies with the service.
- **Nobody, with nothing to gain.** The check runs as the namespace's unprivileged user, with no group, no capabilities, no
  new privileges and a keyring of its own, under a small system-call filter: no sockets of any family, and no keyring,
  io_uring, native asynchronous I/O, bpf, perf or userfaultfd calls.
- **Not run unless made.** Without root, on another architecture than x86-64, without the control groups, on a machine whose
  file table is below 2^40, or if the launcher refuses, the check is not run, and the verdict is UNKNOWN, "the check was
  not run".

## F17

F17 shows that two failures whose notices would once have had the same file name both reach the engineer. To make that
case, it sends two failing designs whose hashes begin with the same eight digits, just after a second begins, so that both
runs end within that second. It tries six pairs.

In the official run, in each of the six tries, the second run ended in the second after the first: the notices were named
for different seconds, and the case F17 exists to make was never made. Its first two checks, that both failed in one second
and that each has its own notice of the shared name, failed for that reason. Its third check passed: the inbox held one
notice for every failure, twelve, and the record verified with all of them. Nothing in the run shows a notice written over
another; nor does it show that one would not be.

The declaration said that F17 had failed in this way in two development runs, for a cause not found, and that both had
begun within minutes of a pass of deliberate defects. The official run began 25 minutes after the last such pass, so that
was not the cause either. Each run now makes and throws away a sandbox; alone, that takes about a tenth of a second, and in
the six full runs before the two that failed, and five runs made to look for the cause, both runs ended in the same second
at the first try. When F17 fails, each of its runs takes about a second. Why is still not known.

F17 is a test of the notices, which this amendment does not change; what it needs of the sandbox is speed. A test that makes
its case by timing can fail to make it, and here it did. Making F17 make its case another way, or the service's runs
quicker, would be a change to a declared test or to the code, and so a separate, dated amendment.

## What changed on the way

The code went through eight forms before the declaration. Six reviews were made, each of the fixes of the one before,
except the fourth, which read the whole of the fourth form. Two findings of high weight in the last two were these:

- the check was the first process of its namespace, and a check that ran itself again from a second thread lost its death
  signal and outlived a service killed during its run (the fifth review). The first process is now the sandbox's own;
- before that, the run's namespaces were made by root, so one check could take root's share of what the kernel counts per
  user, and one run could fill the machine's file table (the fourth review, weighed medium).

The sixth review was cut short when the agents' usage limit for the week was reached: one of its two reviewers finished,
and none of its findings was put to anyone asked to refute it. Its findings were weighed by the author of the code alone.
The first process, as the sixth form made it, was read to the end by no reviewer, and the seventh and eighth forms' changes
were read only by their author. The declaration lists every finding and what was done with it.

A pass of 32 deliberate defects on the eighth form caught all 32, and the control passed. Along the way, four defects were
missed, one of them twice, by checks that could not see them on this machine, or because a later fix covered them twice;
each check was made to see its defect, or the defect to take out every cover, and each was then caught.

## What stays open

The declaration's "What stays open" section is the full list. The main items are:

- The sandbox is the kernel's, not a machine's: a flaw in the kernel's other calls would be a way out.
- The service runs as root, and the demonstration's services share one machine.
- The memory group counts most of what a run uses, not all; some of the kernel's limits are the machine's. Those found are
  dealt with; others may remain.
- The sandbox needs x86-64, cgroup v1, and a file table of at least 2^40 (`sysctl -w fs.file-max=9223372036854775807`, not
  kept across a restart of this machine). Without them, every run is UNKNOWN.
- A check that imports an installed package, or needs /proc or multiprocessing, no longer runs. None in the repository does.
- F17, above.
