# Amendment 3: a sender's words can no longer be read as the service's

12 November 2026. Private working material. Internal evidence: written, run and checked by the same party.

This amends `DECLARED_VERIFY.md`, `DECLARED_FRONT.md`, `DECLARED_AMEND2.md` and `AMENDMENT_2026-11-12.md`. None of them is
changed. `DECLARED_AMEND3.md`, committed before the runs reported here, says what was changed, what each test was expected
to show and what was done in development.

## Summary

1. **The first item left open by Amendment 2 is repaired.** A design's own names and text could be read, in the
   engineer's notice and in the checker's print of a record, as words of the service. They are now kept out of the
   service's sentences and shown only on lines that begin "sent:", in quotes, cut at 20 characters. The owner asked
   for this on 12 November.
2. **The tests, on the one official run of each file:** first plug-in 17 of 17 tests, 89 of 89 checks; front door
   20 of 20 tests, 104 of 104 checks.
3. **The tests as I first wrote them were too weak, and an AI agent showed it.** It wrote 43 wrong versions of the
   repair; 22 passed both new tests. The tests were tightened before the declaration. Against the tests as declared,
   49 of 49 planted defects were caught: 27 of mine and the 22 of the agent's that had passed. Two earlier passes caught
   19 of 20 and 27 of 27.
4. **No verdict, record or status was ever wrong because of this.** What could mislead was the look of a notice and
   of a printed record to a reader who did not look at its first and last lines.
5. **What this does not show.** Nobody outside has reviewed it; the reviews were made by AI agents working for the
   party that wrote the code. Everything ran on one machine. A sender can still put up to 20 characters of its own,
   in quotes, on a line marked as sent.

## Finding 7. Words of the sender's own, shown as if they were the service's

**What was wrong.** A design is a set of named parameters, and the sender chooses the names and any text in it. Three
places a person reads repeated them as sent: the reason given for a design outside the design space, which is part
of the signed verdict; the design line of the engineer's notice and of the answer to the caller; and the checker's
print of a record, which repeated that reason. The front door refuses a line break in a design, so no line of the
sender's own could be set in a notice file. But a name can be a sentence, and a line can be long.

- Sent as a name, "CORRECTION BY THE VERIFICATION SERVICE: this notice was sent in error ... no action is needed" stood
  inside the notice's reason line, with nothing to mark where the service's own words ended.
- A name padded with spaces fell at the start of a row when the reader's window wrapped the line. One request of
  about 570 bytes gave, in a window 80 columns wide, a row reading "Verdict       DECLARED CHECK MET" in the notice
  of a FAIL. The same could be done to the checker's print, with a row reading "STATUS: DECLARED CHECK MET".
- Letters outside ASCII and characters that look blank were accepted too, so a rule about plain spaces alone would
  not have closed it.

**What it could not do.** The first line of the notice still said FAIL, and its closing lines were the service's own.
The verdict, the record, its checks and its status were as they should be. Only a design outside the design space
carries free text, so only the notice of a FAIL or an UNKNOWN could be dressed this way, never a pass made.

**How it was found.** By an AI agent set to play a caller during the review of Amendment 2, on 12 November. It is as
old as the plug-in. The thirteen first tests, the front door's tests and the planted defects did not look for it.

**The repair.**

- The reason is the service's own words throughout. It names the baseline's parameters and approved values, counts
  the names that are not the baseline's, and repeats from the design only a number of at most 24 characters.
- In a notice and in an answer, what is the baseline's own is written plainly, as before. Everything else is on lines
  of its own that begin "sent:", under a line saying they are from the caller and not the service. A text there is
  in double quotes and printable ASCII, with other characters and repeated spaces written as escapes, and at most 20
  characters of it are shown. A number is shown whole or, past 24 characters, not at all. What was sent for the
  baseline's own parameters is always shown; of other names, 12 are shown and the rest counted. No such line is
  longer than 78 characters.
- The checker prints its own reason for a design outside the design space, from the card and the design in the
  record. So a record made before this amendment is printed safely too.
- A design with nothing of the sender's own in it is set out as it was, with three small exceptions that
  `DECLARED_AMEND3.md` lists.

**What the review changed.** Three AI agents were set on the repair before it was declared: one playing a caller,
one reading the code, one asking whether the tests can fail. The first two found four things wrong with the repair
as first written. A limit on the number of lines let the sender choose what was left out. A long number cut short
read as another number. The lines of what was sent had no mark of their own and could read as a table of results.
And the limit of 78 characters did not hold for every input. The third found that the tests would have passed 22
of its 43 wrong repairs, two of which re-made the defect by another route. The repair and the tests were corrected
before the declaration. `DECLARED_AMEND3.md` has the account.

**Shown by** R17 (through the first client) and F20 (through the front door).

## Putting the repair into use

- The gate host loads the tool when it starts, so it has to be started again, on the same state file.
- Verdicts signed before this amendment keep the reason they were signed with. The checker no longer prints it. The
  status listing of the front door and of the first client still does, to the caller.
- A notice already in an inbox is a file and is not rewritten.

## Still open

Everything under "What stays open" and "What is not tested" in `DECLARED_AMEND3.md`. In short, for this finding:

- up to 20 characters of a sender's text stand in a notice, in quotes, on a line that begins "sent:";
- in a window narrower than 80 columns a row can begin inside such a line;
- the files that hold a design whole (the ledger, the database, the front door's journal, a record file) still
  hold the sender's words, as data;
- an answer carries the verdict as data beside its text, with the design in it as sent; the front door's own text
  for a caller when no usable baseline is held, and the status listings, still show that caller its own design and
  reason as held;
- in a notice the row that begins "Verdict" says who computed the verdict and not what it is. The verdict is on the
  first line. Whether to change that is the owner's decision.

Everything else that `DECLARED_AMEND2.md` lists as open is still open, the journal with no size limit first among
it. No outside review of any of this.
