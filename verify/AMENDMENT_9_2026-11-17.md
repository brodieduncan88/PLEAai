# Amendment 9, 17 November 2026

Private working material. Times are UTC.

## What it repairs

Three of a reviewer's points on the record, sent by the owner on 17 November. `DECLARED_AMEND9.md` states the changes, the
expected outcomes, the development work and what stays open.

In short:

- **A pass is called the declared check met, wherever a person or an assistant reads it (point 1).** The text of a verdict has
  what the check does not cover directly under its headline, as well as at its end. The status listings and the engineer's
  console show a run that met the check as MET, and say, beside a design met, that this does not say the check is correct, the
  requirement is complete or the design is safe. The front door's field is `declared_check_met`, not `verified`, and its
  answer carries what the check does not cover. The tools' descriptions say the same. The signed verdict is still PASS, so every
  record made so far still verifies.
- **A second baseline for a requirement is linked to the first, in the signed card (point 3).** The engineer's console refuses
  a second baseline for a requirement id the service holds, unless it is a revision or the engineer gives a reason. With a
  reason, the card names every baseline held for that requirement, and the reason; the engineer signs it with the rest of the
  card, and the record's checker and both clients print it. Ids that differ only in case, spacing, characters that show
  nothing or the kind of dash are one requirement, and a link is never taken from the draft.
- **The gate's check of an inbox is named for what it shows (point 4).** What Amendment 8 called a proof of delivery is now an
  inbox-writability check: WRITABLE, UNCHECKED, STALE or NOT_WRITABLE, kept in `inbox_checks/`, and the front door's refusal is
  `INBOX_UNCHECKED`. The console says that it is not delivery.
- **A run left pending gets its notice with no one sending its design again (point 4).** The front door looks for runs left
  pending on its jobs and, after two minutes and only while the inbox check is good and the budget open, asks for each itself,
  at twice the wait each time up to an hour, and at most twelve times. The gate host still never runs one twice: a run it lost
  is answered with its signed UNKNOWN and noticed as interrupted. A run cut off whose notice is missing is given it, held or
  not, and in fixed words where the journal does not hold its design.

## What changed on the way

The code went through four forms before the declaration. The first full runs found F2 stopped in its set-up: it baselines one
requirement twice at one service, which the new rule refuses without a reason. F2 was given one, and so was the trials'
preparation, which does the same for each trial's job. A first pass of deliberate defects showed three that stopped R23 with an
error where a check should have failed, and R23 was changed so that a check fails.

Three independent reviews were made, each of the fixes for the one before. The first, of the first form, reported nine things;
the most serious were that the continuity rule could be dodged with an id that prints alike, that a link written in the draft
was signed, and that the look for runs left pending would ask for a run it could not finish for ever, adding to the audit log,
and read every job's runs every 30 seconds. The second reported fourteen, five of medium weight, all in the look and the rule;
the third, asked for findings of medium weight or more, reported five. Each was fixed, or is stated as open, and the
declaration lists them one by one. F35 was written for the look, and R23 was given three more checks for the rule. The fourth
form, which the declaration carries, was not itself read by a reviewer.

On the fourth form, both suites passed in development with the counts declared, and the pass of deliberate defects caught all
48, with the control passing.

## What stays open

The declaration's "What stays open" section is the full list. The main items are:

- The record is still evidence of process, not of correctness. The new wording says so in more places; it cannot make a wrong
  check right, and that a reader is not misled by it is not tested.
- A record still does not show that it is current, and a notice, and the inbox-writability check, are still written by the
  service they report on. Both need a statement by the witness, held by a second party: the reviewer's points 3 and 4 in their
  first halves, with D2, D7 and D11 in the register. That is a separate amendment.
- Continuity is linked only within one service, and only by the console. A relaxed requirement given a new id, or baselined
  at another service, is not linked; a card made and signed by other means is not refused by the gate; and a folder put in by
  someone who can write the service's home is counted.
- The look for runs left pending runs only while the front door runs, asks only for runs whose design the journal holds, and
  leaves a run it cannot finish after twelve asks, with no notice that it has. Through the first client, which has no front
  door, a run cut off still gets no notice.
